# Idea 1 — The incumbent (filter-builder modal, ported as-is)

## Why we are exploring this

We aren't. This is the design Neil rejected on 2 September, ported unchanged so the other three
directions have something real to be measured against. Showing it again is a direct response to his
own complaint that Sprint 2 arrived as "the conclusion without the working" — if we drop it from the
set entirely, we're still showing him one option with the others as unstated background. Keeping it
in, labelled as the rejected baseline, is how we make the comparison he asked for actually visible
rather than asserted. Its evidence section below is therefore not a case *for* it — it's the record
of *why it lost*.

## The evidence — why it was rejected

Ordered by how directly each quote drove the rejection, strongest first. All quotes from *Shoreditch
Design Sync: Design and Tech*, 2 September 2026 (Neil Dodgson, Jack, Richard Barnett, Prabal Saha,
Austin Joseph present), unless marked otherwise. Timestamp not available in transcript — Granola's
transcript for this meeting is undifferentiated running text with no per-utterance timing, so every
entry below carries no timestamp; this is a limitation of the source, not an estimate.

1. **"I'm just a bit concerned that it's a bit fiddly when you... as soon as you hit the modal
   environment versus kind of having the full pane to work with."** — Neil Dodgson, *Design Sync*,
   2 Sep 2026. Timestamp not available in transcript.
   *Implication: this is the specific mechanical complaint. A contained overlay, however well built,
   reads as fiddly next to a full pane — the modal shape itself is the problem, not the content
   inside it.*

2. **"I feel like on the trees I lose the context. I can't have multiple things open at the same
   time, so I lose functionality. So I'm not a fan of the trees, I suppose."** — Neil Dodgson,
   *Design Sync*, 2 Sep 2026. Timestamp not available in transcript.
   *Implication: the manual filter tree's central failure — one branch visible at a time — is named
   explicitly. Any successor has to let more than one branch stay open.*

3. **"I'm not sure how much we've solved for in the advanced [tab]... Feels like we're delivering a
   similar number of clicks to the way we have it today."** — Neil Dodgson, *Design Sync*, 2 Sep
   2026. Timestamp not available in transcript.
   *Implication: measured against his own first criterion (number of clicks), the manual tab didn't
   move the needle versus the live product.*

4. **"Is there... do we maybe make this a full-screen takeover? So rather than... having this pop up
   from the screen below it, this can just be like a full screen element."** — Neil Dodgson, *Design
   Sync*, 2 Sep 2026. Timestamp not available in transcript.
   *Implication: he proposed the fix himself, on the call. This is the direct client mandate behind
   Idea 2's shape, not a designer's inference.*

5. **"Can I ask you a question on this? ... One is to place it modal, right, versus kind of like tray
   out from the left... I'm just wondering the thinking behind that."** — Neil Dodgson, *Design
   Sync*, 2 Sep 2026. Timestamp not available in transcript.
   *Implication: he questioned the modal-vs-tray decision on the call rather than endorsing it — this
   was a live design choice he wanted justified, not a settled preference of his.*

6. **"It's a conclusion without— you've almost presented the conclusion without the working... [we
   need] us giving you feedback on the work."** — Neil Dodgson, *Design Sync*, 2 Sep 2026. Timestamp
   not available in transcript.
   *Implication: the more damaging complaint wasn't about the modal at all — it was that Sprint 2 was
   the only path shown after four weeks. Idea 1's presence in this set, labelled as the discarded
   baseline, is the direct answer to this specific line.*

7. **For balance — he did not reject it outright:** *"But it's a definite step up from where we were
   last time you showed me."* — Neil Dodgson, *Design Sync*, 2 Sep 2026. Timestamp not available in
   transcript.
   *Implication: the fiddliness/context-loss complaint is about the interaction inside the modal, not
   a verdict on the underlying content or filter logic — worth carrying that logic forward even
   though the modal shell isn't.*

## What this direction is betting

That re-showing the rejected design, honestly and without softening it, is worth the risk of Neil
reading it as "they didn't listen." The bet is that a working, in-context comparison — see it, then
see the other three next to it — lands better than a written recap of what was wrong. If he's already
moved past Sprint 2 and doesn't want to see it again in any form, this backfires.

## What we would need from the client to validate it

Ask directly: does showing the incumbent alongside the three new directions satisfy the "show the
discarded options and the reasoning" request from 2 September, or does re-surfacing it read as
relitigating a decision he considers closed?
