Four routes to
natural language search

Four clickable prototypes, the pros and cons of each, and a link into every state of every flow. For review before Wednesday.

GlobalData × Shoreditch · September 2026

The four routes

Each one takes a different position on how much of the search experience the AI should take over. The range is the point. None of them is a recommendation yet.

01
Modal / Sidebar Takeover
The design reviewed on 2 September, rebuilt so it runs. Filtering is a mode you enter. One overlay, two tabs, one shared builder. Present as the baseline the others are measured against, not as a candidate.
02
Miller Columns
The taxonomy drills sideways instead of down. Each level opens beside the last, nothing is wiped, every row carries the count it would leave you with. An agent drives those same columns in place.
03
Text Input Field
Natural language takes the whole stage. The query comes back as one editable line of English: every value a pill with a dropdown, every operator a word you can change.
04
Sidebar Agent
No filter surface at all. You land in the grid and filter from the column headers, Excel style, with a docked assistant acting on the same grid. The only route that redesigns the results view.

What we are solving for

Three user goals, and the four measures you set on Monday. Every route in this deck is scored against them.

G1 · Quick screen
Most searches, most users. A cold start that reaches a usable list fast. You arrive knowing what you want to screen for, and go for it.
G2 · Refine
Power users holding a list that is nearly right. Cheap, reversible, legible refinement without starting the query again.
G3 · Deep Boolean
Advanced users at your largest accounts. AND, OR, NOT across attribute groups, with nesting. The granularity they are paying for.
70% of queries
Use three fields or fewer. This is the group that needs the fast on-ramp.
10% of refined queries
Need the detailed Boolean builder, and they come from your biggest accounts.
Clicks and speed
Number of clicks to a list, and how quickly the list arrives.
Three terms or fewer
70% of queries have to be reachable inside that budget.

Where each route lands

Click counts read out of each prototype's own code, counting the handlers a user has to fire. The live platform today takes 13 to 16 clicks for the same three-field query, then commits blind for six seconds.

01 · Modal
12 clicks by hand, 4 by natural language. Boolean is drawn but not honoured: between-group OR is ignored by the filter. Serves G2.
02 · Miller
6 clicks by hand, 2 by the agent in 4.7 seconds. AND, OR and NOT inside an attribute, no cross-attribute OR. Serves G2, part of G3.
03 · Text field
Zero clicks. One sentence and Enter returns three conditions and 23,200 drugs. Flat AND only, and nesting is silently flattened. Serves G1.
04 · Sidebar
6 clicks plus a dismissal, 2 by the assistant. The thinnest query model of the four: no NOT anywhere. Serves G2.
Every route beats the live platform on the quick screen
Three of the four reach a three-field list in six clicks or fewer, against 13 to 16 today.
No route matches it on deep Boolean
None of the four builds a group of ANDs set against another group. Your platform does.
Counts before you commit
All four price a value before you spend the click. The live platform shows no number anywhere.
That trade is the decision
Speed for the 70% against expressive power for the 10%, and which route closes the gap fastest.

01 · Modal / Sidebar Takeover

In favour
What it does well
  • +Natural language and hand-building write into one object. A pill the AI wrote reopens the same picker path a hand-built one does.
  • +Counts sit next to every value before you spend a click on it.
  • +The applied query stays editable from the pill bar, with no Apply step to repeat.
  • +Boolean appears as visible controls rather than typed syntax. HubSpot and Proton ship the same shape.
Against
What it costs
  • —The cold start runs to 3n plus 3 clicks. It matches the live platform rather than beating it, so it fails G1 on your first measure.
  • —The modal hides the table and shows no total until Apply. The value counts are corpus-wide constants, not conditioned on what is already picked.
  • —Three stacked cascade levels, one branch open at a time. Your own objection, and the research agrees with it.
  • —Between-group OR is drawn and then ignored by the filter, and NOT prefixes the next group rather than joining it. No parentheses. It fails G3.
  • —A second question replaces the query instead of amending it, so the AI tab can start a query but never refine one.

Verdict. The route for G2, someone nudging one value on a list they already have. Its single structural problem is that filtering is a mode that hides the results it filters, so the fast start and the visible depth sit behind the same door.

02 · Miller Columns

In favour
What it does well
  • +The path stays on screen and nothing is wiped. This is the "I lose the context" complaint answered directly, and what the live screener gets wrong.
  • +Counts on every attribute and every value, recomputed with that attribute's own condition lifted.
  • +No commit step. Count, pills and table derive from one filtered set, so they cannot disagree.
  • +The filter surface is independent of the grid. It opens filtered on an attribute no column shows.
  • +The agent drives the real controls, step by step, each step undoable, and yields the moment you touch a column.
Against
What it costs
  • —There is no cold start. The composer sits at the foot of the panel, below the taxonomy, so the slow instrument is in front of you and the fast one is below the fold.
  • —Nothing sequences the task. One value row has three outcomes depending on which pixel you hit.
  • —It runs at roughly half the real density, 15 attributes against 27, so the bet is untested at full size.
  • —Three columns only render above 1880px. At your 1440px review window you get two.
  • —Attributes are hard-ANDed, so cross-attribute OR, grouping and nesting have no path. G3 is not served.

Verdict. The strongest structure of the four and the weakest opening. Keep it, move the composer from the foot of the panel to the front of the journey, and let the sentence land in the columns. The first query and the lesson in how the taxonomy works then become the same event, which is the one thing Option 3 cannot do.

03 · Text Input Field

In favour
What it does well
  • +The cold start is genuinely zero clicks. The composer is focused on arrival and Enter submits.
  • +The prompt resolves into controls you can see, which is the documented remedy for not knowing what you are allowed to ask.
  • +Edits are priced right. Two clicks to correct a value, one to drop a condition, one undo step per edit.
  • +The only route that answers your question with one object. The Filters view imports its controls from the sentence, so the swap is provably lossless.
  • +It uses the natural language search you already built in the header and currently orphan.
Against
What it costs
  • —It misreads queries in ways that read as correct. "Marketed in Germany but not approved in Japan" comes back as "only in Germany or Japan, not in the Marketed stage". Fluent, confident, wrong.
  • —Users will not check. Measured trust in these tools runs 6.1 out of 7 against 4.0 for a native interface, with light verification.
  • —A blank input teaches nothing. Four suggestions against 27 real attributes. Option 2 shows you all of them.
  • —Nested Boolean is not expressible, and the parser flattens it silently rather than refusing.
  • —No way to refine from the results. The grid sorts and nothing more.

Verdict. The fastest cold start of the four and the only single-object answer to the natural language question, but it tops out at your current flat Boolean ceiling. The right front door for the 70%, not yet a home for the 10%. What closes it is a third rung: Sentence, then Filters, then a nested group builder, all on one query object, with the composer live inside the builder so a typed phrase adds a group at any depth.

04 · Sidebar Agent

In favour
What it does well
  • +The only route that answers the grid complaint, and the fix is portable underneath any of the other three.
  • +Two clicks per parameter with scope-correct counts, so the zero-hit query stops happening.
  • +The Excel idiom at the top level, which is what your users have in their hands all day.
  • +Propose, preview the row count, land the steps, leave a receipt with an undo. The textbook shape for an agent that edits data.
  • +Column management the live product lacks: nine of 37 columns shown, each hideable, movable and pinnable.
Against
What it costs
  • —The cold start, which is your objection. The build opens with three filters already applied, hiding the real first screen: every row in the database and nine headers to guess at.
  • —This pattern has the worst retention record of any here. Tableau retired Ask Data in 2024 and Power BI's Q&A goes in February 2027.
  • —You can only filter what is on screen. Drug name and brand are not filterable at all, and the sort and group menus list visible columns only.
  • —The thinnest query model of the four. OR within a column, AND across them, and no NOT anywhere. It fails G3 outright.
  • —Undo is whole-grid, so it reverts hand edits made since, and the panel eats 380px of a grid whose problem is width.

Verdict. The grid redesign is the most valuable single artefact in Sprint 3 and should be adopted whichever filtering model wins. The results-first filtering model, as built, fails the cold start you named, so it continues only with a screener on its front and Boolean moved into the filter pills.

What we need from you

01
Which front door?
A sentence that resolves into structure, or a browsable taxonomy with a sentence on its front. Both reach a three-field list in under six clicks. They teach the user completely different things on the way.
02
Does the grid redesign go in regardless?
Option 4's results view answers a complaint none of the others touch, and it is independent of which filtering model wins.
03
How much Boolean do the 10% actually need?
No route expresses nested, cross-attribute logic today, and building it is real work. We need your read on what the largest accounts genuinely construct before we design for it.
04
What we would do next, unprompted
Fuse Options 2 and 3. A composer at the front that resolves a sentence into the columns, the columns as the power view it lands in, and a group builder underneath for the 10%. Both halves already run.
